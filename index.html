Washburn Roshambo

Every student starts with a blue necklace and every teacher with a gold one. Two people play rock-paper-scissors, and the best-of-3 winner takes necklaces from the loser. People don't agree on the other rules, so pick some and see where the necklaces end up.

One challenge: best 2 out of 3

Ties are replayed and don't count.

A challenge uses the current rules and changes the population below. Click people in the population grid to fill in the IDs.

Things to try in class
  • Winner takes all: how fast do the necklaces pile up on a few people? How many people end up with nothing?
  • Gold first vs. blue first: which rule moves gold necklaces into students' hands faster?
  • Teachers can't challenge students: does it protect students, or teachers?
  • Zero necklaces can still challenge: people with nothing to lose keep playing. What does that do to the big winners?
  • Keep the same seed and change one rule at a time, so the only difference is the rule.
How the simulation works
  • Time moves in ticks, each one 5-second game slot. A person plays at most one game per tick and no more than the "Games per person" limit (25 minutes ÷ 5 s = 300).
  • A challenge is directed: the challenger picks a target. The target must have at least one necklace to lose.
  • "Teachers CANNOT challenge students" still lets a student challenge a teacher. Pick "never play each other" to keep them apart completely.
  • If someone with no necklaces challenges and loses, nothing changes hands.
  • Any setup change starts a new run. The same seed and settings always give the same result.
